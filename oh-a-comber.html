Your sample library, finally searchable.

Oh a Comber scans the folders you already have and turns them into a fast, filterable library. It never moves, renames, or rewrites a file.

Why this exists

Oh a Comber indexes your files where they sit. Twenty packs deep, every producer owns the same folder: thousands of one-shots with names like SD_808_F#_140. Most sample managers fix that by importing everything into a library format of their own. This one reads what your filenames and metadata already say, and leaves every file where it is.

Scan Library

Point it at your sample folders, several at once if you like. It indexes .wav, .aiff, .mp3, .flac, .ogg, .m4a, .wma, and .aac, reading duration, sample rate, channels, and BPM from each file's metadata and name.

Indexing, not importing. Your folder structure stays exactly as it is. Exact duplicates are detected by content and hidden automatically, never deleted.

Type & Genre

Every sample gets a Type: kick, snare, hihat, clap, cymbal, tom, percussion, bass, synth, keys, guitar, strings, brass, vocal, fx, or loop. Type is read from the filename and folder path. Pack names map to genres the same way.

It's transparent pattern-matching on the names your packs already carry, not a black box. You can predict what it will decide, and re-scanning a new pack takes seconds.

Search & filters

Search matches filename, path, and pack. Stack it with Type chips, Genre, a BPM Range, and ★ FAVORITES. The counts update live.

"kick", 80–100 BPM, one pack name: six thousand files become five candidates while the last one is still playing.

Preview & audition

Click play and the waveform draws in the player bar; click the waveform to seek. Arrow keys walk the list and audition each sample as you move.

Auditioning is the whole job. You never leave the list, and you never stop listening.

Drag out to your DAW

Rows drag like files because they are files: dragging hands your DAW the original path on disk. Right-click reveals the file in Finder.

No export step, no copies, no managed library between you and the sound. Search, preview, drop it on a track.

INSTRUMENTS

Load any sample onto the KEYBOARD and play it chromatically across 24 keys, or bank nine one-shots on the DRUM PAD. Play them with the mouse, the computer keyboard, or a MIDI controller.

Sketch the part before you commit the sample. C3 keeps the original pitch; the pads sit on MIDI notes 48–56.

Questions

Does it reorganize my folders?

Never. Scanning builds a local database that points at your files where they already live. Nothing is moved, renamed, copied, or rewritten. Delete the app and your library is exactly as it was.

What happens with duplicates?

Files with identical content are detected by hash. One copy stays visible in the library; the rest are hidden from lists and counts. The files themselves are never touched.

Will drag-and-drop work with my DAW?

Dragging out of Oh a Comber is a native OS file drag carrying the sample's real path. It works with anything that accepts an audio file drop: Live, Logic, FL Studio, Reaper, or a plain Finder window.

Do I have to re-scan when I add new packs?

Yes. Click Scan Library again. Existing entries update in place and your favorites survive the re-scan.

Is there a trial? What will it cost?

Pricing isn't decided yet, and there's no public build to try today. The waitlist gets first access and the pricing announcement before anyone else.

Does any of my library leave my machine?

No. There are no accounts, no sync, and no telemetry. Your samples and the index database stay on your machine.
