iPad chord instrument · one key, one chord

Press one key. Get a full chord.

Oh a Chord is a one-octave iPad keyboard where a single key press plays a full chord. Chord Type and Modifier buttons choose its quality, Key Mode keeps every note inside the key you pick, and a held chord turns into a strum, a harp sweep, or an arpeggio without ever retriggering.

See how it works

Why this exists

Most chord and arpeggiator apps ask you to already know some theory before they're any fun — the right scale, the right extensions, an arpeggiator wired up by hand. Oh a Chord skips straight to the sound: press a key, hear a chord, hold a couple of fingers down to color it, and let Key Mode keep you inside a key without ever needing to know why. The whole instrument is built around one idea — a musician with no theory knowledge should make a good-sounding progression within ten seconds of opening it.

One key, a full chord

A single octave sets the root. Chord Type buttons choose Dim, Min, Maj, Aug, Sus2 or Sus4, and Modifiers — 6, ♭7, maj7, 9, 11, 13 — stack colour on top for as long as a finger holds them down.

No chord charts, no fingerings to memorize. Every Chord Type and Modifier combination resolves to a real, playable chord — there's no dead button mid-performance.

Key Mode

Arm Key Mode and the next key you press becomes the root. Every press after that stays inside that key — reach outside it and Oh a Chord borrows a sus or diminished chord that still belongs, instead of going silent.

It's music theory you can feel rather than study — a way to build a good-sounding progression without knowing why it works yet.

Strum, Harp, Arp

Three independent toggles turn a held chord into a part: Strum staggers the notes like a pick across strings, Harp sweeps one or two octaves, Arp cycles up or up-and-down at the tempo you set. Latch holds the chord hands-free.

A held chord doesn't have to just sit there. These are the same moves a hardware arpeggiator gives you, without ever retriggering the notes underneath.

A full synth underneath

Behind the gear icon is the same 6-voice DSP core that powers Oh a Synth — oscillator, filter, envelopes, chorus — every parameter live and touchable, without ever leaving the keyboard behind.

The one-key chord is the front door, not the whole house. Anyone who wants to shape the sound itself can go as deep as Oh a Synth goes.

Save your own sounds

Tweak anything in the synth editor and a save icon appears. Tap it, name the sound, and it joins the preset list — right there next to the factory bank, stored on the device.

A good sound found by accident shouldn't be lost the next time you touch a slider.

Questions

Do I need to know music theory to use this?

No — that's the whole point. Press a key, hear a chord. Key Mode is there for anyone who wants to explore a key without knowing why a particular note works, not a requirement to get started.

Does it work with MIDI?

Not yet. MIDI output is planned but not in this release — Oh a Chord is fully playable and self-contained today with no setup needed.

Can I save my own sounds?

Yes. Open the settings gear, change anything in the synth editor, and a save icon appears — name it and it's added to the preset list alongside the factory bank.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. Nothing about Oh a Chord ever leaves the device — no accounts, no analytics, no sync. Saved presets are a file on your iPad, nowhere else.

What iPad do I need?

iPadOS 16 or later. It's a landscape-only instrument — chord controls under your left hand, keyboard under your right.
