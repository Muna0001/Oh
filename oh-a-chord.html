iPad chord instrument · one key, one chord

Press one key. Get a full chord.

Oh a Chord is a one-octave iPad keyboard. One key press plays a full chord. Chord Type and Modifier buttons choose its quality. Key Mode keeps every note inside the key you pick. A held chord turns into a strum, a harp sweep, or an arpeggio, and never retriggers.

See how it works

Why this exists

Oh a Chord goes straight to the sound. Press a key and hear a chord. Hold a couple of fingers down to colour it. Key Mode keeps you inside a key without needing to know why. Most chord and arpeggiator apps want theory first: the right scale, the right extensions, an arpeggiator wired up by hand. This one asks for none of it.

One key, a full chord

A single octave sets the root. Chord Type buttons choose Dim, Min, Maj, Aug, Sus2 or Sus4. Modifiers (6, ♭7, maj7, 9, 11, 13) stack colour on top for as long as a finger holds them down.

No chord charts, no fingerings to memorize. Every Chord Type and Modifier combination resolves to a real, playable chord. There are no dead buttons mid-performance.

Key Mode

Arm Key Mode and the next key you press becomes the root. Every press after that stays inside that key. Reach outside it and Oh a Chord borrows a sus or diminished chord that still belongs. It never goes silent.

It's music theory you can feel rather than study. Build a progression that works before you know why it works.

Strum, Harp, Arp

Three independent toggles turn a held chord into a part: Strum staggers the notes like a pick across strings, Harp sweeps one or two octaves, Arp cycles up or up-and-down at the tempo you set. Latch holds the chord hands-free.

A held chord doesn't have to sit there. These are the same moves a hardware arpeggiator gives you. The notes underneath never retrigger.

A full synth underneath

Behind the gear icon sits the same 6-voice DSP core that powers Oh a Synth: oscillator, filter, envelopes, chorus. Every parameter is live and touchable. The keyboard stays where it is.

The one-key chord is the front door, not the whole house. Shape the sound as deep as Oh a Synth goes.

Save your own sounds

Tweak anything in the synth editor and a save icon appears. Tap it and name the sound. It joins the preset list next to the factory bank, stored on the device.

A good sound found by accident shouldn't be lost the next time you touch a slider.

Questions

Do I need to know music theory to use this?

No. That's the whole point. Press a key, hear a chord. Key Mode is there if you want to explore a key without knowing why a particular note works. It isn't required to get started.

Does it work with MIDI?

Not yet. MIDI output is planned but not in this release. Oh a Chord is fully playable and self-contained today, with no setup.

Can I save my own sounds?

Yes. Open the settings gear and change anything in the synth editor. A save icon appears. Name it and it joins the preset list alongside the factory bank.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. Nothing about Oh a Chord leaves the device. No accounts, no analytics, no sync. Saved presets are a file on your iPad and nowhere else.

What iPad do I need?

iPadOS 16 or later. It's landscape only. Chord controls sit under your left hand, keyboard under your right.
